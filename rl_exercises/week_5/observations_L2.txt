Level 2: Empirical Understanding

1) Effect of trajectory length

Configuration:
- algorithm = REINFORCE
- environments = CartPole-v0 and CartPole-v1
- learning_rate = 0.01
- gamma = 0.99
- hidden_size = 128
- episodes = 1000

Observation:
The trajectory length had a clear effect on the stability of training.
In CartPole-v0, where the maximum episode length is shorter, the agent reached the maximum evaluation return of 200 several times and became quite stable in the later part of training.

In CartPole-v1, the agent also reached the maximum return of 500 several times, but the performance was less stable and dropped again after successful phases.

This suggests that longer trajectories can provide more reward information, but they also increase the variance of Monte Carlo returns.

Since REINFORCE updates the policy from full episode returns, longer trajectories can make training noisier and less stable.

2) Same architecture and learning rate on LunarLander

Configuration:
- algorithm = REINFORCE
- environment = LunarLander-v3
- learning_rate = 0.01
- gamma = 0.99
- hidden_size = 128
- episodes = 1000

Observation:
The same network architecture and learning rate did not work reliably for LunarLander.
The agent sometimes achieved good evaluation scores, for example around 263 at episode 650, but the learning was highly unstable and the performance later dropped again to negative returns.

This shows that LunarLander is more difficult than CartPole and that the same hyperparameters do not transfer reliably.

The environment has a larger observation space, more actions, and a more complex reward structure.
A smaller learning rate, more training, or variance-reduction methods would likely be needed for more stable learning.

3) Sample complexity compared to DQN

Observation:
REINFORCE appears to be less sample-efficient than the DQN implementation from the previous exercise.

DQN uses a replay buffer and can reuse past transitions many times during training.

REINFORCE, on the other hand, is an on-policy Monte Carlo method, so each trajectory is used only for the current update and then discarded.

This means REINFORCE usually needs more environment interaction and is more sensitive to noisy returns.

In practice, DQN learned CartPole more consistently, while REINFORCE showed stronger fluctuations and unstable performance, especially on the longer CartPole-v1 task and on LunarLander.
