
Setting:
In this experiment, we study whether low-fidelity evaluations can be misleading in RL hyperparameter optimization.

Algorithm: tabular Q-learning
Environment: stochastic chain environment
Low fidelity: 50 training episodes
High fidelity: 250 training episodes
Seeds: 0..4
Metric: mean greedy evaluation return

Why early scores can be deceiving:
In reinforcement learning, early performance is noisy. A configuration can look good after only a small training budget because it exploits quickly. However, another configuration may need more training before it becomes strong. Therefore, selecting configurations only from early performance can negatively influence multi-fidelity optimization.

Results:
The best low-fidelity configuration was config 2:
low_mean = 0.996
high_mean = 0.996
high-fidelity rank = 2

The best high-fidelity configuration was config 0:
low_mean = 0.792
high_mean = 0.996
low-fidelity rank = 17

Several configurations changed rank strongly. For example, config 0 looked mediocre after 50 episodes, ranking only 17th under low fidelity, but became the best configuration after 250 episodes. This shows that early performance did not perfectly predict final performance.

Conclusion:
The results show that low-fidelity evaluations can be misleading in RL. The configuration selected as best after 50 episodes was not the same as the configuration selected as best after 250 episodes. Therefore, a multi-fidelity optimizer that promotes only configurations with strong early performance could miss strong final configurations. This supports the idea that multi-fidelity HPO in RL should be used carefully, with multiple seeds and final high-fidelity validation.
